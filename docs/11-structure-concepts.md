# Five architectures, and the one that got built

The previous structure was hero, marquee, claim, statement, technique, object,
who-it-is-for, comparison, why-us, practitioner, FAQ, close. Restyled, better
paced, but describable in one sentence: hero, then features, then proof, then
FAQ. That is the failure. What follows is five genuinely different structures,
not five variations.

## 01 — Editorial storytelling
Long-form. Chapters with their own covers. "Why you gave up on the last one",
"What water actually does", "The forty seconds". Product appears late as the
resolution of an argument.
**Gains** narrative pull, no other flosser site reads like this.
**Loses** speed to purchase. A reader who already wants one has to wade.

## 02 — Product-first
The object is the narrator. Everything hangs off it: hover a part, that part
explains itself. Tank, nozzle collar, mode strip, grip.
**Gains** the object is genuinely interesting and we have the CAD to do it.
**Loses** answers "what is it" but never "is it for me", which is the actual
question in this category.

## 03 — Luxury pacing
Minimal, selective, high whitespace, one idea per screen. Bang & Olufsen.
**Gains** premium feel, which the SRS asks for.
**Loses** the technique story, which needs room. And luxury pacing on a $79
product for a Gen Z buyer reads as pretension rather than confidence.

## 04 — Discovery, non-linear
The reader self-selects and the page answers them. No fixed path.
**Gains** relevance. Nobody in this category does it.
**Loses** SEO surface and simplicity if the whole page is behind a choice.

## 05 — Conversion engine
Trust, price, guarantee, action, all early. Everything else subordinate.
**Gains** decisiveness.
**Loses** the only reason this brand exists. Selling it fast is how the
category creates people who abandon it in a month.

## The hybrid

Take the **admission** as the opening move, **self-selection** from 04 as the
second, the **correction** as the third, and only then the object from 02. Keep
05's decisiveness at the close and 03's pacing throughout.

The order stops being a page and becomes a consultation:

    ATTENTION    the object, and one sentence
    ADMISSION    most people abandon these within a month
    DIAGNOSIS    which of these is you, and what that means for you
    CORRECTION   the technique that decides whether it works
    CLARITY      what it does and does not replace
    DESIRE       the object measured
    EVALUATION   against floss and against mouthwash
    TRUST        warranty, returns, what we refuse to claim
    DECISION     price, box, guarantee

Two structural consequences, both deliberate:

**The admission moves to position two.** The page tells you the category fails
before it tells you anything good. Nothing else in this market opens that way,
and it is the honest version of a hook: it earns the next scroll instead of
demanding it.

**Three sections merge into one interactive moment.** Who it is for, the five
nozzles, and the four modes were three separate lists of the same information
sorted three different ways. As a diagnosis they become one question with a
personal answer: your tip, your mode, your first fortnight.

## The test

Strip the logo and the colours. The structure still reads as unusual, because
it opens by admitting the product usually fails and then asks the reader a
question before it sells them anything. That is a structure, not a template.
